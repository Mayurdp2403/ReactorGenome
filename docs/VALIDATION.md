## C. Conventions that differ between the reports, the manuals and the app

**Λ∞ (conductivity at complete conversion)**

- Reports / manuals: CSTR manual 3.75 mS/cm; PFR manual 0.1 mS/cm.
- App: Default Λ∞ = 0.366 Λ0 from limiting ionic conductivities (Na⁺ 50.1, OH⁻ 198, OAc⁻ 40.9 S·cm²/mol); a typed constant is available.
- Why it matters: X = (Λ0 − Λ)/(Λ0 − Λ∞), so a lower Λ∞ gives a lower X. 0.1 mS/cm is essentially pure water and understates conversion; 3.75 mS/cm matches the ion estimate only near Λ0 ≈ 10 mS/cm (B1).


**Steady state in the CSTR**

- Reports / manuals: Average of readings after the first, or the last reading.
- App: Plateau fitted from the readings with the simulated start-up shape (default), a single exponential, or the manual average; the user picks the rule that decides 'steady'.
- Why it matters: Averages of readings that are still moving bias X toward the start value (B2).


**Tanks in series from an RTD**

- Reports / manuals: N = τ²/σ² (report) with τ = V/Q.
- App: N = tm²/σ² by default; τ²/σ² shown next to it.
- Why it matters: They differ whenever tm ≠ V/Q, i.e. whenever there is dead volume or a cut-off tail.


**Open-vessel Peclet number**

- Reports / manuals: σ²/tm² = 2/Pe + 8/Pe² (report).
- App: Shown, together with the exact open–open form σ²/tm² = (2/Pe + 8/Pe²)/(1 + 2/Pe)² and the closed form.
- Why it matters: The short form treats the measured tm as τ; the exact form accounts for tm = τ(1 + 2/Pe).


**Closed-vessel Peclet number**

- Reports / manuals: –
- App: σ²/τ² = 2/Pe − 2(1 − e^−Pe)/Pe².
- Why it matters: An earlier version of the app had + in place of −; corrected and tested.


**PFR intercept**

- Reports / manuals: Free intercept in τ against X/(1−X).
- App: Modelled as reaction time after the outlet (sample delay).
- Why it matters: See B3.


**Batch order**

- Reports / manuals: Second order assumed; differential method gave n ≈ 1.
- App: Order chosen by the user, compared by AIC, with a clock offset.
- Why it matters: See B4.


**Settling time**

- Reports / manuals: Not modelled; readings at fixed times (for example τ, τ+1, τ+2 min in the timed CSTR set).
- App: Simulated for the chosen start: from water the slow mode is τ; after a flow change it is τ(1−X)/(1+X).
- Why it matters: After a flow change the tank settles faster than τ suggests; from water it needs several τ.

### D4. What the readings say about Λ∞

| set | Λ∞/Λ0 fitted | 95% interval | residual (mS/cm) | change between flows ÷ residual | k with that Λ∞ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CSTR | 0.300 | 0.252 to 0.349 | 0.026 | 17.9 | 31 °C: 8.48, 46 °C: 25.7, 60 °C: 42.7 |
| PFR | 0.575 | 0.489 to 0.660 | 0.191 | 2.1 | 40 °C: 104, 50 °C: 240, 60 °C: 392 |

If the reactor is ideal, every run at one temperature shares one k. Fitting a single Λ∞/Λ0 and one k per temperature to all nine CSTR runs gives Λ∞ ≈ 0.30 Λ0, close to the 0.366 Λ0 expected from ion conductivities (sodium acetate replaces sodium hydroxide) and far from the 0.1 mS/cm (0.01 Λ0) the report uses. With that Λ∞, k at 31 °C is about 8.5 L/mol/min, in the range usually quoted for this reaction near 30 °C, where the report's 3.16 is low. The single-run k falling as τ grows in the report (3.38 → 3.00 at 31 °C) is what a Λ∞ that is too low produces; with Λ∞ = 0.30 Λ0 the same three runs give 8.8, 8.7 and 8.1. The PFR readings scatter by up to 1 mS/cm between repeats, more than they change between flows, so they cannot fix Λ∞; the app says so instead of reporting the fit.

### D6. What the app adds beyond the reports

- Observation table for every run (Λ average, steady Λ used, X, y, single-run k), as in each report's Table 2, built as the readings are typed.

- Three ways to fit k (y on τ, τ on y, free line) with 95% intervals on k and on Ea; parity and residual plots.

- Single-run k against τ: a flat line means the data are consistent; a slope points to Λ∞, a delay before reading, or unsteady runs.

- Λ∞ estimated from the readings, with an interval and a check of whether the data can support it.

- Steady-state check for CSTR runs when reading times are typed: plateau extrapolation from the simulated start-up and a per-run verdict.

- A non-ideal model of each reactor (bypass, dead and stagnant volume, tanks in series, dispersion, laminar flow, recycle, sample delay, reversible reaction, energy balance) fitted or compared against the same readings.

- RTD: all four runs on one sheet, E(θ) overlay, N rounded and unrounded, three Peclet conventions, τ from Pe, D_L from bed geometry, tail correction with the tail's share of the tracer, model fits, and the conversion each vessel would give.

- k and Ea fitted on one rig can be used to predict another rig or a designed network (Design Studio).

- Every page exports a report (HTML), the tables (CSV) and every plot, and the whole session saves as a project file.


## E. What was not validated

- No COMSOL, Aspen or Simulink models were run for comparison; only their modelling principles were followed.

- The jacket energy balance is checked against its isothermal limit and for multiple steady states, not against measured temperatures.

- Coil Reynolds and Dean numbers use the water viscosity correlation and the coil geometry typed by the user.
